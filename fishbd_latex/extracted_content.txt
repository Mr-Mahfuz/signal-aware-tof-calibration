1. Introduction

Bangladesh is strongly connected to inland and open-water fisheries through rivers, ponds, beels, floodplains, haors and other aquatic habitats. The Department of Fisheries identifies inland fisheries as a major national resource and reports 260 freshwater fish species in the country. This diversity creates an important opportunity for computer-vision research, but it also creates a data-quality challenge: a recognition system is only as dependable as the images and labels used to train and evaluate it.

Fish identification from images is difficult because species can share similar body shapes, colors, fin structures, scale patterns, and visual features. Lighting, water condition, background, camera angle, fish orientation, and image quality can further change the appearance of the same species. Existing Bangladesh-focused datasets show that image-based fish recognition is already an active research direction. For example, BD-Freshwater-Fish contains 4,389 images of 12 species, while a later smartphone-based dataset contains 24,925 images of 21 freshwater species. 

FishBD is proposed not simply as a larger collection of pictures, but as a curated research resource in which species identity, source information, image quality, metadata, and validation status are treated as linked components. The project will combine field/source collection, taxonomic verification, annotation, preprocessing, and AI-assisted quality screening into one reproducible pipeline.

Why this topic is important in the Bangladesh context

Fisheries have a direct national importance: the Department of Fisheries reports 2.53% contribution to national GDP and 22.26% to agricultural GDP in 2023–24. 

Bangladesh contains substantial freshwater fish diversity, making local rather than generic international datasets important for local recognition tasks. 

The national Red List assessment included 253 freshwater fish species and identified conservation concerns associated with habitat loss, overexploitation, invasive species, pollution and climate change. 

Computer-vision systems can support identification and monitoring, but their reliability depends strongly on representative images and correct labels.

Students, researchers and fisheries-related users need datasets that can be inspected, reused, and understood through clear metadata rather than opaque collections of images.

Scope of the proposed work

Focus on Bangladeshi freshwater fish species selected according to feasibility, availability and taxonomic relevance.

Collect or curate images representing multiple environments and realistic visual variation rather than a single controlled background only.

Attach scientific name, local name, source/habitat information, image identifier and verification status to each accepted image.

Use ML/DL as an assistive screening and benchmarking component, while keeping human/taxonomic verification as the final authority.

Produce documentation and dataset statistics so future researchers can understand how the dataset was constructed.

2. Literature Review / Background Study

Recent Bangladesh-focused work demonstrates strong interest in fish image classification, but the studies also reveal why dataset construction remains a meaningful research problem.

Research gap. The literature shows that Bangladesh already has useful fish-image resources, so FishBD should not claim that no dataset exists. The more defensible gap is methodological: existing resources differ in species scope, collection conditions, metadata depth and validation procedures. FishBD therefore focuses on creating a transparent dataset-development framework in which taxonomic verification is explicitly documented and ML/DL is used for quality assistance rather than treated as a substitute for taxonomic expertise.

Taxonomic reference basis. FishBase currently lists hundreds of fish species for Bangladesh and provides scientific classification and identification-oriented information. The IUCN Red List of Bangladesh separately documents the conservation status of freshwater fishes. These sources can support the reference layer used for scientific names and conservation context. 

3. Problem Statement

Bangladesh has substantial freshwater fish diversity, but image-based fish recognition is constrained by the quality, representativeness and reliability of available training data. Existing datasets are valuable but vary in species coverage and collection design. Images may come from markets or controlled backgrounds, and the same species may appear differently under changes in -

Image quality. 

The central problem addressed by FishBD is therefore the absence of a clearly documented, taxonomically verified and quality-controlled dataset-development pipeline that connects image collection with authoritative species identity, metadata, AI-assisted screening and human feedback. Without such a process, an apparently accurate ML/DL model may learn from mislabeled, duplicated, biased or visually unrepresentative examples.

Research question

How can a Bangladesh-focused fish image dataset be constructed so that species labels are taxonomically defensible, image quality is controlled, metadata are standardized, and ML/DL can assist—rather than replace—human verification?

4. Objectives

Design and implement a reproducible pipeline for collecting and curating images of selected Bangladeshi freshwater fish species.

Verify each accepted species label against recognized taxonomic references and expert feedback, recording the verification status as dataset metadata.

Apply standardized quality control, duplicate screening, annotation and preprocessing so that the final dataset is suitable for machine-learning and deep-learning research.

Develop an ML/DL-assisted screening stage that flags low-confidence, visually inconsistent or potentially mislabeled samples for human re-checking.

Produce a documented benchmark-ready dataset with defined training, validation and testing partitions, along with dataset statistics and quality reports.

These objectives are specific to dataset development, measurable through image/species counts, verification records, quality-control outcomes and documented validation procedures, achievable with open-source tools, relevant to Bangladesh's fisheries and AI context, and planned within the 12-month project period.

5. Proposed Methodology / System Design

Stage A — Source and image collection

Collect images from feasible sources such as field locations, fish markets, fisheries-related contacts, institutional collections and openly reusable sources, subject to permission and usage conditions.

Record source, approximate collection context, date where available, local name, provisional identification and image identifier.

Seek variation in orientation, distance, lighting, background and body presentation so that the dataset does not represent only one photographic condition.

Reject images whose origin or reuse rights cannot be reasonably established for the intended dataset.

Stage B — Taxonomic verification

Taxonomic verification will follow a hierarchy. First, the provisional species identity will be compared with authoritative taxonomic resources such as FishBase and relevant Bangladesh biodiversity/fisheries references. Second, morphological characteristics visible in the image will be checked against the expected species description. Third, uncertain cases will be reviewed by a knowledgeable human reviewer or taxonomic expert where available. FishBase provides a Bangladesh species list and classification fields, while the IUCN Bangladesh freshwater-fish volume provides species-level conservation context.

Stage C — Quality control and annotation

Remove exact or near-duplicate images where they could cause data leakage.

Flag or remove severely blurred, overexposed, underexposed, obstructed or unusable images.

Maintain a consistent image naming convention and unique image ID.

Store scientific name, local/common name, source/habitat, location when appropriate, verification status and other available metadata.

Keep rejected-image reasons in a quality-control log rather than silently deleting evidence of the curation process.

Stage D — ML/DL-assisted screening

After a sufficiently verified subset is available, a baseline classifier can be trained using CNN architectures or a Vision Transformer (ViT)-type model. The purpose at this stage is not to declare the taxonomic truth automatically. Instead, the model can identify samples that deserve additional inspection.

 How the ML/DL component works

Input: verified training images are converted to a consistent image format and size.

Preprocessing: normalization and carefully selected augmentation can reduce sensitivity to harmless changes in image presentation.

Feature learning: a CNN learns spatial patterns such as edges, textures, body shape and fin-related features; a ViT-type model can learn relationships among image patches.

Prediction: the model outputs class probabilities or a confidence score for each candidate image.

Screening: low-confidence predictions, disagreement with the recorded label, or unusual feature patterns are flagged.

Human feedback: flagged cases are sent back to the verification stage; the reviewer can confirm, correct, or reject the image.

Iteration: corrected labels can be used in a controlled re-training cycle, while verification records remain separate from model predictions.

Verification feedback mechanism

The feedback mechanism is intentionally conservative. A model disagreement is treated as a signal for inspection, not proof that the human label is wrong. If the taxonomic reference and expert review support the existing label, the image remains accepted even when the model is uncertain. If the reviewer finds an incorrect label, the metadata are corrected and the sample is reprocessed. This separation helps prevent circular validation in which a model is allowed to validate the same assumptions used to train it.

Dataset organization and validation

Create training, validation and test partitions at the specimen/source level where possible, reducing the chance that near-identical images of one fish appear in multiple splits.

Report class distribution, image counts, source distribution, image-quality statistics and verification status.

Document preprocessing and augmentation so that future researchers can reproduce the benchmark.

Maintain a final audit checklist before dataset release.

6. Method Justification

The proposed methodology fits the project because the core deliverable is a reliable dataset, not merely a classification model. A dataset-first approach allows the project to address label correctness, diversity, metadata quality and reproducibility before measuring AI performance.

The three members will work without a separate team-leader role. Responsibilities are divided by primary ownership, while verification decisions and major dataset changes are discussed jointly.

Team management principle: each member maintains a change record for their work, and no major species-label change is accepted into the final release without cross-checking the supporting evidence.

11. Timeline

The schedule is deliberately overlapping because collection, verification, quality control and annotation are iterative. Field availability and expert-review timing may require adjustments without changing the overall 12-month completion target.

12. Cost-Benefit Analysis

The project is designed as a low-cost research activity because it can use existing smartphones/cameras, open-source software and available computing resources. The main variable cost is expected to be local travel and field collection.

Benefit assessment. The main benefit is a reusable research asset: a verified and documented dataset can support future fish classification, detection, biodiversity studies and student research without repeating the entire data-collection process. This makes the proposed approach economically reasonable relative to repeatedly building small, disconnected datasets.

13. Constraints and Limitations

Economic

Travel budget may restrict geographic coverage.

Specialized equipment or distant collection sites may increase cost.

Environmental

Weather and water conditions can affect field collection.

Some species may be seasonally available or difficult to photograph.

Ethical and social

Permission may be required for photographs or institutional sources.

Images and metadata must respect source ownership and responsible data sharing.

Technical

Poor lighting, occlusion, blur and background variation can reduce image quality.

Class imbalance may occur when some species are easier to obtain than others.

Taxonomic

Some images may not contain enough diagnostic features for confident identification.

Expert availability may be limited, so uncertain samples may remain unresolved rather than being forced into a class.

Health and safety

Field collection near rivers, ponds or boats may involve physical hazards.

Team members should follow safe collection and travel practices.

Research: researchers can reuse standardized images and metadata for classification, detection and biodiversity-related studies.

Fisheries: reliable visual references may support future digital tools for fish identification and monitoring.

Conservation: better species records can contribute to computational biodiversity studies, particularly where conservation status is already documented by national assessments. 

Community value: a structured digital resource can connect local names and scientific names and make fish-related information easier to organize.

Responsible AI: separating taxonomic authority from model prediction reduces the risk of presenting an AI guess as biological truth.

15. Complex Engineering Problem Mapping

FishBD maps to the complex engineering problem domains because the work is not a single coding task. It combines biological knowledge, data engineering, image processing, AI, field constraints, stakeholder interaction and validation requirements. The strongest domains are WP1, WP2, WP3, WP4, WP6 and WP7.

Why this mapping matters. The project becomes an engineering problem because improving one part can affect another. For example, increasing species coverage may increase verification workload; increasing image diversity can improve realism but may reduce model performance if the data become noisy; stricter verification can improve reliability but increase project time. The proposal therefore requires trade-off decisions rather than a single fixed algorithm.

16. Complex Engineering Activity Mapping

The proposal aligns most strongly with EA1, EA2, EA3 and EA4. EA5 is also relevant because the project combines familiar programming techniques with less predictable field and taxonomic situations.

Why we chose this engineering-activity mapping

EA1 was selected because FishBD cannot be built with programming alone; it needs multiple resources and evidence types. EA2 was selected because the reliability of the dataset depends on interaction between collectors, data curators, taxonomic reviewers and AI researchers. EA3 was selected because the proposed contribution is a workflow innovation that joins taxonomic verification with ML/DL-assisted quality screening and an explicit feedback loop. EA4 was selected because the project is connected to fisheries, biodiversity and responsible environmental practice. EA5 is relevant because the team will encounter species that are easy to identify as well as cases where images are insufficient or conflicting. The project must therefore investigate rather than assume.

Appendix B — Final Verification Decision Rules

Do not accepted a species label only because an ML model predicts it with high confidence.

Do not forced an uncertain image into a species class when the visible evidence is inadequate.

Use authoritative taxonomic references and human review to establish the final label.

Use ML/DL predictions to prioritize difficult cases, detect inconsistencies and support benchmarking.

Keep a traceable record of corrections and rejected samples.

Separate dataset curation from final model evaluation to reduce circular validation.
